You lose the hour back. Northwest Indiana is on Central time like Chicago. The clock jumps forward about forty miles past the state line, near LaPorte — not at the border. Everything below is Eastern from that point on.

The Route

I-80 the whole way once you’re past Chicago. Seven pieces, and the fifth one is most of the night.

Checked against Culver’s own location list: there are none in Pennsylvania, New Jersey, or New York. Ohio is the end of the line.

The Overnight Problem

Ohio and Indiana are easy

Both toll roads have service plazas on the mainline every 30–40 miles, and the fuel and bathrooms are open all night. You never have to leave the road. Hot food shrinks to one counter after midnight, but coffee is always there.

Pennsylvania is the thin part

I-80 across PA has no mainline service plazas at all. Everything is an exit ramp, and between about 2 and 5 AM a lot of those exits have nothing open. The reliable ones are the truck stops: Brookville (exit 81), DuBois (exit 101), Snow Shoe (exit 147), Milton/Williamsport (exit 212), Bloomsburg (exit 232). All 24 hours, all with real coffee and a bathroom you’d use.

Snow Shoe to Milton is the emptiest 65 miles of the night. Fill up before it, not after.

Where to swap drivers

The natural handoff is the Ohio–Pennsylvania line, about five and a half hours in. Whoever takes PA gets the hard shift: four and three quarter hours, dark, hilly, and dull. Swap again at DuBois or Snow Shoe if that’s too much.

The last hour — the bridge, the Cross Bronx, the Throgs Neck — should be whoever is most awake, not whoever’s turn it is.

The Shape Of It

Thirteen and a half hours moving, closer to fifteen and a half on the clock once you count the lost hour and two real stops. Leave Geneva at 6 PM Central and you’re on Long Island around 10:30 AM Eastern. Leave at 8 PM and it’s half past noon.

The good news about going overnight: you cross Chicago, Cleveland, and the George Washington Bridge at hours when none of them are a problem. That is the entire argument for driving through the night, and it is a good one.
