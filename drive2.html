Pennsylvania

0:15

Kittanning and the Allegheny

PA-28 north · out the left window

You’re driving up one of the old highways of North America. Kittanning was a major Lenape town, and the Kittanning Path over the mountains was the main east-west trail through Pennsylvania long before any road existed. The river beside you is still run by a chain of locks and dams from the barge era.

0:25

Ford City — the town that made plate glass

Along the river

Named for John B. Ford, who built the plate-glass works here. The company he helped start became PPG. For a long stretch, a serious share of America’s window glass came out of this valley.

1:10

The Emlenton bridge

I-80 west · you drive over it

I-80 crosses the Allegheny here on one of the highest bridges on the whole interstate — the town of Emlenton sits in the gorge underneath you. Blink and it’s over, but look down and you’ll see rooftops.

1:20

Where the oil industry started

40 miles north · Titusville

You’re skirting the bottom of the world’s first oil region. In 1859, Edwin Drake drilled the first commercially successful oil well near Titusville, and the global petroleum industry started in these hills. The boomtowns are mostly gone; some are literally ghost towns now.

1:30

Punxsutawney, off to the south

Behind you now

Phil’s town, about 25 miles south of the highway. You passed the same junction coming in on Thursday.

2:20

Ohio line — the turnpike starts

Take a ticket

I-80 becomes the Ohio Turnpike. You pay at the far end, so hang on to it.

Ohio

2:35

Youngstown — and Black Monday

South of the road

Steel town. On 19 September 1977 — still called Black Monday here — Youngstown Sheet & Tube announced closures that ended about 5,000 jobs in a single day. The city lost more than half its population over the following decades. Springsteen wrote a song called “Youngstown” about exactly this.

The valley you’re crossing is the reason the phrase “Rust Belt” exists.

2:45

Warren — where Packard came from

Just north

James Ward Packard built his first car here in 1899, reportedly after being unimpressed with a Winton he’d bought and being told by Mr Winton that if he thought he could do better he should go build his own. He did.

3:15

Akron, and a river that caught fire

South · Cuyahoga valley

Akron was the rubber capital of the world — Goodyear, Firestone, and the tyres on most American cars for most of a century. Alcoholics Anonymous was founded there in 1935.

And the Cuyahoga River, which you cross, was so polluted it caught fire — most famously in 1969. That fire helped push through the Clean Water Act and the creation of the EPA. The river is clean enough for fish now.

4:00

Lake Erie, and a teenage commodore

North · Sandusky

Cedar Point is out on its peninsula north of you — more roller coasters than almost anywhere on earth. Further out on the lake, in 1813, Oliver Hazard Perry beat a British squadron and sent the line every kid should get to say out loud: “We have met the enemy and they are ours.” He was 27.

4:30

The Great Black Swamp

You are driving across the bottom of it

This is the best answer to “why is it suddenly so flat.” Northwest Ohio was a swamp the size of Connecticut — roughly 1,500 square miles of standing water and hardwood forest, left behind by a glacial lake. It was close to impassable. Settlers went around it for decades; it’s why this corner of Ohio was settled so much later than the rest.

It was drained in the 1800s with ditches and clay tile, and the swamp floor turned out to be some of the richest farmland in the country. Every flat black field you can see was underwater.

4:45

Toledo — and the war over it

Around you

Glass city: Libbey and Owens made Toledo the centre of American glassmaking, and Jeep has been built here since the war.

Better story: in 1835 Ohio and Michigan nearly fought a war over this spot. Both claimed the Toledo Strip, both sent militia, and there was a lot of marching and almost no shooting. Congress settled it — Ohio kept Toledo, and Michigan was handed the Upper Peninsula as a consolation prize. Michigan thought it had been robbed. Then they found iron and copper up there.

Indiana

5:15

You just gained an hour

Eastern → Central

Phones will flip on their own. Indiana’s time-zone map is genuinely strange — the northwest corner runs on Chicago time, most of the state runs on New York time, and for years much of it refused to observe daylight saving at all.

5:30

Elkhart makes almost every RV in America

Right along the toll road

Somewhere around four-fifths of the RVs in the United States are built within a few miles of here. Watch the shoulder — you’ll start seeing brand-new ones being driven out in convoys. The town was also a band-instrument capital; a lot of school trumpets came from Elkhart.

5:45

South Bend — Notre Dame and Studebaker

South of the road

The Golden Dome and the stadium are a couple of miles off the highway. Studebaker built wagons and then cars here for over a century — they made wagons for the Union Army before they ever made an automobile — until the plant closed in 1963 and took the city’s economy with it.

6:20

Gary, and the biggest steel mill in the country

Both sides · unmistakable

The industrial skyline coming up is Gary Works — built by US Steel starting in 1906, on empty dunes, with a whole city laid out around it and named after the company’s chairman. At its peak it was the largest steel mill on earth. It still runs.

Gary is also where the Jackson family lived; Michael was born there in 1958, in a two-bedroom house.

6:30

Sand dunes, in between the mills

North · Lake Michigan

Indiana Dunes became a national park in 2019 — big Lake Michigan dunes wedged directly between steel mills and refineries. One of the odder juxtapositions in the park system, and one of the most biodiverse places in the Midwest.

Illinois

7:00

I-88 is the Ronald Reagan Memorial Tollway

The last leg

Named for him because he grew up out this way — born in Tampico, raised in Dixon, both further west along this same road.

7:20

Fermilab — and its bison

Batavia · minutes from the house

America’s great particle-physics lab is right next door to where you’re sleeping. The top quark was discovered here in 1995. From the road you can see Wilson Hall rising out of the prairie like something that landed.

And there is a herd of American bison living on the site, kept there since 1969 — the founding director started it as a link to the prairie the lab was built on. You can drive in and look at them, free. That is a genuinely great thing to do with a five-year-old tomorrow.

7:30

Aurora, the City of Lights

Arrive

On 8 November 1881, Aurora switched on sixteen electric arc lamps mounted on seven towers 150 feet tall — enough, in theory, to light the entire city electrically, when most of America was still on gas. It took the nickname “City of Lights” officially in 1908.

Also, and Max may care more about this: Wayne’s World is set in Aurora. Party on.
